# Typographic introduction

**Problem:** A visitor needs to understand a purpose, value, or next decision before acting.

**Why it belongs:** Clear scale, deliberate line breaks, warm open space, and precise alignment express the identity without ornamental chrome. Departure Mono can give a short statement distinctive character while coherent reading text explains it.

**Appropriate:** Product discovery, an unfamiliar tool's starting state, or a focused explanation before a consequential task.

**Avoid:** Repeating a large introduction above every working screen, stretching a trivial label into a hero, or burying frequent actions beneath explanatory marketing content.

**Acceptable variations:** Compact or expansive; centered, asymmetric, or aligned with surrounding content; real product imagery/evidence when useful; short mono display or a justified reading-family long title. Content and available space determine the shape. No fixed hero/CTA/feature-section sequence is implied.

**Annotated decision:** If a new visitor needs to understand a local-first product, give its specific capability and limitation readable prominence before choosing an action. Existing users returning to perform work may need only a compact title and immediate task access. Both preserve the same type-role and surface logic. A generic slogan with decorative machine graphics solves neither need.
