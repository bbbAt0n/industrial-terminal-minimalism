# Ruled information group

**Problem:** Related information needs legible grouping, scanning, or comparison without unnecessary containers.

**Why it belongs:** Thin functional rules, aligned labels/values, and controlled spacing provide the identity's precision and restraint. Hierarchy remains in content and typography rather than repeated shadows/badges.

**Appropriate:** Specifications, comparisons, settings groups, meaningful metadata, or a related data region. Use table semantics when row/column relationships matter.

**Avoid:** Turning unrelated objects into a table, ruling every sentence, replacing an independently interactive item/card with an ambiguous row, or stretching rows across unreadable measures.

**Acceptable variations:** Open groups, labeled lists, description lists, actual tables, or bounded interactive objects when needed. Row density and column relationships follow content/platform. Decorative rules may be quieter than essential boundaries; numbers and units need consistent alignment.

**Annotated decision:** Comparable product specifications can share a ruled comparison structure. A set of independent projects may instead need individually actionable objects. Removing every card is no more principled than adding one to every metric; the meaningful relationship decides.
